Storm water: roof, window or flood?

Rain from a storm enters in several ways, and each is handled differently. Roof leaks and window failures bring in rainwater. Runoff entering through a basement door is a flood. The difference affects cleanup and insurance, and you should describe what happened as exactly as you can.

Hurricane Irene in August 2011 also caused extensive flooding in Maryland, according to the Maryland Manual. Storms like these are why storm water calls come in clusters, and why crews prioritize by safety and severity.

After the storm

Stay out of rooms with water near outlets or a panel. Avoid downed lines, and call the utility. Do not walk onto a damaged roof. Wear boots and gloves in standing water, because storm water can carry contamination.

Wind-driven rain can travel into attics and walls without leaving obvious marks. The ceiling often shows it last.

Ice, wind and the second leak

The first storm opens the roof, and the next rain makes it worse. A tarp buys time, but wind lifts them, so check it after each weather change. If a window failed, board it so rain and wind cannot push through, and keep the photos of the damage before you cover it.

Wind-driven rain can enter at the wall and flow down inside it. The only reliable way to find that water is a moisture meter, since the wall looks fine from outside and the stain appears later on the ceiling below.

If trees or limbs hit the house, stay clear of the area until it is safe. Do not climb onto a damaged roof, and call a roofer for the permanent repair. We handle the water, the drying and the documentation.

What to keep for your insurer

  • Photos and video of the opening, the wet areas and the weather.
  • Receipts for tarps, boards and emergency repairs.
  • A list of damaged belongings.
  • Drying logs and moisture readings from the restoration company.
  • Notes on dates, times and who you spoke to.
