Signs your floor is leaking from below

A slab leak is hard to see because the pipe is buried in or under concrete. You see its effects instead. Water rises through cracks, flooring cups or lifts, carpet feels damp, and a hot spot appears on the floor when a hot-water line is leaking.

Check for these before you assume the problem is elsewhere:

  • A water meter that moves when all fixtures are off.
  • Warm or hot patches on the floor.
  • The sound of running water with everything off.
  • Damp carpet, cupped hardwood or a musty smell near the floor.
  • A water bill that jumps without a change in use.

To test, turn off every fixture and watch the meter. If it still moves, water is leaking somewhere, and if shutting the main stops it, the leak is on the house side.

Why slab leaks are different from other leaks

In a wall leak, the water shows up at the wall. In a slab leak, water travels through the concrete, along the gravel bed beneath it and up through joints and cracks. It can wet flooring across a room from a pinhole you cannot see. The damage often covers more area than the pipe repair does.

Concrete holds water longer than most materials. A slab can look dry on the surface and release moisture for days, which is why moisture readings are taken at several points over time instead of once.

Flooring decisions after a slab leak

Carpet and pad that got wet from a clean supply line can sometimes be dried. Hardwood and laminate often cup or swell and may need replacement. The hardwood floor page and the carpet page explain how that call is made.

What a slab leak costs you if you wait

A leak under a slab runs around the clock. Water is wasted, flooring is soaked from below, and the moisture reaches baseboards and the bottoms of walls. A plumber who locates the leak early often limits the area that has to be opened.

Keep the water bill, the meter reading photos and any plumber report. They show your insurer when the leak started and what it affected. Photograph flooring changes before anything is lifted.

Do not tile or lay new flooring over a slab that has leaked until it has been tested dry. Trapped moisture lifts adhesive and causes new cupping, and the repair gets done twice.

Drying a slab and the flooring above it is measured work under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Concrete in particular needs repeated readings, since a dry surface can sit over a wet slab.

Cost and insurance for slab leaks

Cost depends on the flooring involved, the area, drying days and rebuild scope. Coverage varies a lot because insurers treat gradual and sudden leaks differently. The Maryland Insurance Administration says coverage turns on your policy language, so read it and ask your insurer. Call us for a number tied to your house.
