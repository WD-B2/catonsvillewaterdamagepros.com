Where Elkridge's water problems come from

Elkridge is an unincorporated community in Howard County. The 2020 census counted 25,171 people in its census-designated place, and the 21075 ZIP code area is larger, with about 35,900 people in its ZIP code tabulation area. The community stretches along US 1 and I-95, with I-195 reaching it at Exit 3 and MD 100 crossing the north.

The Patapsco Valley State Park borders North Elkridge, so the river and its tributaries are close to part of the community. Water here comes from plumbing and appliances in most calls, and from storms and runoff when a heavy rain hits.

Howard County rules that apply

Elkridge is in Howard County. Building permits come from the Howard County Licenses and Permits Division, and on public sewer the county's Bureau of Utilities is the first call for a sewer problem, before a plumber. Baltimore County and Baltimore City use different offices, so a quick check of the jurisdiction saves time.

Plumbing work that follows a leak, such as a repaired supply line or a new water heater, is done by a licensed plumber under the permit rules for that county. We handle the water, the drying and the documentation.

Elkridge landmarks and what old buildings mean

Elkridge is home to the Thomas Viaduct, built in 1833, along with the Belmont estate and the Elkridge Furnace. Older structures like these remind us that parts of the community date back generations. Homes near the old village core can have stone or brick foundations, original plumbing and shallow crawl spaces.

Newer townhouse and condo communities along US 1 have slab foundations and shared walls. If you share a wall with a neighbor, a leak next door can reach you. Tell the dispatcher about units above, below and beside you.
