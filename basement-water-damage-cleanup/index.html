How basements flood

A basement is the lowest point in the house, so water finds it. Rain runs down the ground toward the foundation, pushes through cracks and the floor-wall joint, or overwhelms a sump pump. Plumbing and appliance failures send water downhill to the same place.

Storm runoff.Water through windows, doors or the wall joint.Often Category 2 or 3.

Electrical and carbon monoxide risk

Do not enter a flooded basement if the water reaches outlets, a panel or appliances. Gas furnaces and water heaters that were submerged need a professional check before use. Run no gas generators indoors.

Stored items are the hardest part. Cardboard boxes, upholstered furniture and paper should be sorted quickly. Photographs and documents have their own handling rules, which the National Archives explains.

Finished versus unfinished basements

An unfinished basement with a concrete floor dries fast. A finished basement hides wet drywall, insulation and carpet that all need attention. Wall cavities behind paneling and cabinets hold water long after the floor looks clear. A thermal camera and meter find it. See also carpet restoration.

Sump pumps, drains and what to check after the water is out

A sump pump is only as good as its power and its discharge. A pump that runs on house current stops during an outage, which is when storms hit hardest. A battery backup keeps it running, and a high-water alarm tells you before the basement does.

After a flood, check that the pump runs, the discharge line is clear and the floor drain is open. Look at downspouts and grading outside, since water that pools next to the foundation finds its way in. These are maintenance fixes for a drainage contractor or a plumber, not part of the cleanup.

Keep stored items off the floor on shelves or pallets, and avoid cardboard in a damp basement. A hygrometer in the space tells you when humidity stays high, and a dehumidifier running year-round keeps it under control.

Basement jobs that involve outside water or sewage follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 category rules, which is why porous materials soaked in Category 3 water come out instead of being dried in place.

Basement calls also come from residential communities such as Westview Park, where the first question we ask is whether the lower level is finished and whether a sump pump is installed.
