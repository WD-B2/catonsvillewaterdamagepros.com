A sagging ceiling is an emergency

A ceiling that sags or bulges is holding water in the drywall or in insulation behind it. Drywall is heavy when wet, and the paper face can tear. A ceiling that has dripped for days can come down, so people and furniture should stay out from under it.

If water is dripping, set a bucket below it and move belongings. If the light fixture is wet or flickering, leave that circuit off. Do not stand under a bubble of water, and call us before you open it.

Water near ceiling lights and fans

If water drips from a light fixture or a ceiling fan, turn off the switch and the breaker if you can reach the panel dry. Do not touch the fixture. If you smell burning or see sparks, call 911 and the utility before you call us.

Where ceiling water comes from

The stain on the ceiling is where water showed up, not where it started. Water moves along joists and pipes before it finds a spot to drip. Technicians trace the path from the stain back to the source with moisture meters and a thermal camera.

Roof leaks that show up as ceiling damage

Water damage from a leaking roof appears indoors long before anyone climbs up to look. Rain enters through flashing, a lifted shingle or a failed seal, runs along the underlayment and soaks insulation. The ceiling is the last layer to show it, and by then the attic framing may be wet too.

A roofer repairs the roof, and we handle the damage inside. If you have both a roof problem and active storm damage, see storm water damage for how wind and rain openings are handled. Do not wait for a dry day to call, because trapped moisture does not drain on its own.

Baltimore County says building permits are required for additions, alterations and structural modifications to existing dwellings. Ask whether the repair you are planning triggers one, particularly if framing is replaced.

Repair options and trade-offs

  • Stain only, ceiling dry: stain-blocking primer and paint can finish the job.
  • Wet drywall but sound: dry in place with equipment if readings allow.
  • Sagging or soft drywall: cut out and replace the damaged section.
  • Wet insulation above: remove and replace, because it holds water against the framing.
  • Old stain with no moisture: confirm it is dry with a meter, then repaint.

Painting over a stain that is still damp traps moisture and the stain returns. Ask for a meter reading before any repainting. For a structure that stays damp, structural drying is the process that pulls the moisture out.

Wet ceilings and the insulation above them are dried and verified under the ANSI/IICRC S500 approach of measuring moisture, not judging by touch. A reading after the repair confirms nothing wet is sealed in.

Old Catonsville ceilings

The Old Catonsville Historic District includes houses built between 1866 and 1950, and many older homes have plaster ceilings instead of drywall. Plaster is heavy and cracks when wet, and a failing plaster ceiling can fall in pieces. If you have plaster, mention it on the first call so the crew can plan the right protection and repair.
