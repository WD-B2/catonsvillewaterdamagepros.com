The Winters Lane community

Winters Lane is a historically African-American residential community in Catonsville. The Winters Lane Historic District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 2007. It covers about 49 acres along Winters Lane between Frederick Road and the Baltimore National Pike, which the record connects to the old National Road.

The district developed from 1867 to the mid-1940s. It holds about 155 properties, nearly all of them residential, and the record describes it as composed mainly of single-family dwellings with a few churches, former schools and social clubs. Those buildings share a short stretch of road, so one plumbing or storm problem can touch several neighbors.

Rentals, duplexes and shared walls

Baltimore County and St. Ambrose Housing Aid Center have worked to rehabilitate duplex homes on Roberts Avenue in the district, and the county announced progress on the project in June 2019. A county news release says a historic preservation agreement requires the facades, porch structures and window arrangements to be kept.

If a rented home or duplex has water damage, tell the landlord or property manager right away and ask who files with the insurer. A shared wall can carry water to the next unit, so tell the dispatcher about units beside, above and below. Keep any preservation or ownership paperwork nearby in case a rebuild needs review.

Storm drains and county help

Baltimore County asks residents to report clogged inlets and runoff from county roads through BaltCoGo 311. Its Storm Drain Design office takes general flooding questions at 410-887-3711. For certain residential flooding problems the county may offer a Residential Flood Mitigation Grant Program, decided case by case after a drainage review.

Read the county storm drain page for current contacts. The county says it cannot improve private property, so work inside your lot is yours to arrange. We handle the water and drying, not the drainage design.

Insurance and documentation

Photos, video and moisture readings can support a restoration record, and an itemized scope can describe the work. Coverage depends on the cause of the loss and your policy, so ask your insurer. The Maryland Insurance Administration says flood is generally excluded from homeowners policies and needs separate coverage.
