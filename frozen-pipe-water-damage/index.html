When a frozen pipe becomes a water emergency

Water expands when it freezes, and the ice can split a pipe along its length. A frozen pipe often shows nothing until it thaws, because the ice plugs the break. When the temperature rises or the line warms, the plug melts and pressurized water pours out of the split.

That means the flood can start hours after the cold ended, often when no one is looking at the wall. People find it as a stain on a ceiling, a wet basement floor or a sound of running water in a closed wall.

Treat these as signs of a split pipe after a freeze:

  • A faucet that produces no water or only a trickle during freezing weather.
  • Frost on a pipe or a bulge in the pipe.
  • The sound of running water when everything is off.
  • A damp patch on a wall, ceiling or floor under a pipe run.

Cold-weather facts that matter locally

The Maryland Manual reports that the freeze-free period in the state averages 185 days, and the statewide winter average is 34.1 degrees. We do not have a Catonsville-specific count of freezing nights, and we will not make one up. What matters for your pipes is where they run, not the yearly average.

Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages, crawl spaces and basement corners are the ones at risk. A short freeze of a few hours after a long mild stretch can catch a house whose owner has not covered vents or disconnected hoses. Check these places before cold weather, and see the crawl space page if your pipes run under the floor.

What to do if a pipe is frozen but has not split

Step 01.

Open the faucet.

Leave the tap on so water can flow as the ice melts. Moving water helps prevent pressure from building behind the plug.

Step 02.

Warm the area gently.

A hair dryer on low or a warm towel can help. Never use an open flame, a torch or a space heater near the pipe.

Step 03.

Know your shut-off.

Locate the main valve before you start. If the pipe splits, turn the main off immediately.

Step 04.

Call a plumber if it will not thaw.

A pipe you cannot reach or that stays frozen needs a professional. Do not keep heating it blindly.

What cleanup looks like after a thaw flood

The water from a split pipe is usually clean supply water, but a thaw flood often goes unnoticed for hours. By the time it is found, it has saturated insulation, drywall and the floor below. Crews extract standing water first, then open wall cavities and run air movers and dehumidifiers.

The wall where a pipe froze is often an exterior wall with insulation. Wet insulation holds water against the framing and is usually removed. The wall and drywall page explains how cuts, drying and patching work, and structural drying explains the readings that tell you when it is finished.

Do not heat the house to dry it out

Raising the thermostat can feel like the right move, but wet insulation and sealed walls need airflow and dehumidification, not just warmth. Heat can also push moisture deeper before it escapes. Let the readings guide the equipment.

Insurance and cost for frozen pipe damage

The Maryland Insurance Administration says a suddenly burst pipe that floods floors is generally covered for the water damage, though not for repairing or replacing the pipe. Frozen pipe claims turn on whether the house was kept heated and maintained, so read your policy and ask your insurer.
