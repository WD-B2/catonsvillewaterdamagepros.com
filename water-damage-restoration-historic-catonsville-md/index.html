Where Historic Catonsville sits

Historic Catonsville is the older residential core of Catonsville, an unincorporated community in Baltimore County. The Central Catonsville and Summit Park Historic District was added to the National Register of Historic Places on December 27, 2006. The Maryland Historical Trust describes it as bounded by Frederick Road on the north, Rolling Road on the west and south and Mellor Avenue on the east.

The same record calls the district entirely residential, with 349 contributing buildings that date from 1869 to 1958. Summit Park, the subdivision around the 1850s Gary Mansion called The Summit, holds most of the single-family houses. A separate architectural survey describes the wider old neighborhood as large lots and tree-lined streets, so streets just outside the mapped district may feel the same.

What the record says about water here

On May 27, 2018, a storm dropped more than 10 inches of rain on southwestern Baltimore County, according to a county letter quoted by GovTech. The same report says Catonsville and Oella were hit hardest in the county and that storm drains overflowed behind a restaurant on Frederick Road. What the storm does show is that drains and pipes sized for ordinary rain can be overwhelmed. Working in older houses

Houses in the district range from 1869 to 1958, and the National Register record notes bungalows, Foursquares, Colonial Revivals, Cape Cods and ranch houses. Construction in those eras can include plaster walls, solid wood floors and original millwork. We do not assume a house has any of them, but we ask, because those materials dry and fail differently from modern drywall and laminate.

Homes built before 1978 may also contain lead-based paint, and the EPA has lead-safe work rules for disturbing painted surfaces in those homes. Cutting out wet plaster or trim can create dust, so say so early if the house is old. See the EPA's lead information for homeowners.

Insurance and documentation

Photos, video and moisture readings can help document damage, and an itemized scope can describe the work done. Whether your policy covers a loss depends on the cause and the policy terms, so call your insurer and ask. The Maryland Insurance Administration says homeowners policies generally do not cover flood and that sewer or water backup coverage is typically an endorsement.

If the house is on a Catonsville street served by public sewer and the problem is in the street main, Baltimore County's Department of Public Works is the first call. Indoor cleanup is a separate job. Call us when water is already inside.
