About Westview Park

Westview Park is a residential community north of the Baltimore National Pike. Wikipedia's Catonsville entry describes about 1,800 homes developed in stages from 1955 to 1967 and says the community straddles Interstate 695. Listing sites give the same 1955 to 1967 range and show both single-family and attached homes.

Boundaries are loose. Real estate sites label some Westview Park addresses Catonsville 21228 and others Gwynn Oak 21207. This page covers homes with a Catonsville 21228 address, so give your street and ZIP code on the call and the dispatcher will confirm.

Johnnycake Road and Ingleside Avenue run near the community, and the Westview Mall area sits on land that was once part of the Gibson estate. The houses themselves date from the postwar building period, not from that earlier estate.

What a mid-century construction date tells us

The sources give a construction period, not a house type, so we do not assume basements, slabs or crawl spaces. Homes built in the 1950s and 1960s are now roughly 60 to 70 years old, and parts of their plumbing, roofs and water heaters may have been replaced over time.

We do not assume older homes leak more, and we do not assume any plumbing material. We do ask the year, the foundation type and any past repairs. Those details change where water travels and which materials need drying.

Sewage or floodwater is different

Water from a sewer backup or from outside the house can carry contamination. Keep people and pets out, do not use household fans or a vacuum on it and do not wade in water that may touch outlets or appliances. Call the line and describe what you see.

County help and insurance

Baltimore County takes reports of clogged street inlets and runoff from county roads through BaltCoGo 311, and its Storm Drain Design office takes flooding questions at 410-887-3711. For certain residential flooding problems the county may offer a Residential Flood Mitigation Grant Program, reviewed case by case. The county cannot improve private property.

Photos, video and moisture readings can document damage, and an itemized scope can describe the work. Whether your policy covers it depends on the cause and the policy terms, so ask your insurer. The Maryland Insurance Administration says homeowners policies generally do not cover flood and that sewer or water backup coverage is typically an endorsement.
