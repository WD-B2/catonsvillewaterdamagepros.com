About Catonsville Water Damage Pros

We take water damage calls around the clock for Catonsville and nearby towns. This page explains how the line works, what we promise and what we will not claim.

CALL-ONLY LINE.24/7.CATONSVILLE, MD.

What we do

Catonsville Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration service based at 5748 Baltimore National Pike in Catonsville, Maryland. We handle extraction, drying, cleanup and the repairs that follow a leak, a burst pipe, a storm or a flood. You reach us by phone. There is no online form, so a person answers and asks what is happening.

How a call goes

Step 01.

A dispatcher answers.

The line is open 24 hours a day, including nights, weekends and holidays.

Step 02.

We ask the basics.

Where is the water, where did it come from and how long has it been there?

Step 03.

You get first steps.

Shut-off, safety and what to lift or move while a crew is arranged.

Step 04.

Scope and price come first.

We explain what needs doing and the likely cost before work starts.

What we promise

  • We explain the scope and the price before work starts.
  • We take moisture readings and share them, so dryness is measured.
  • We tell you plainly if a job is small enough to do yourself.
  • We send a crew the same day when one is available and say so honestly when it is not.
  • We keep photos and logs your insurer may ask for.

What we will not claim

We do not publish customer reviews or star ratings, because we would rather you judge us on a phone call and the records of your own job. We do not quote a price without seeing the loss. We do not tell you what your insurance covers, since your insurer decides that. When the Maryland Insurance Administration describes common patterns, we pass them along as general information.

Where we work

Our home base is Catonsville, and we also take calls from Ellicott City and Elkridge in Howard County and from Baltimore City. See the service area page for ZIP codes and county differences, and the services overview for the 19 situations we cover.

Why we work by phone

A form sends your words into a queue. A call starts the response. For water damage, the first five minutes decide how much of the house stays dry. The most useful thing we can give you in that time is instructions you can act on, such as where to find the main valve and what to lift off the floor.

That is also why there are no popups, chat widgets or contact forms on this site. The numbers on every page go to the same line.

How we write the guides

The guides on this site cite sources you can check. Insurance statements come from the Maryland Insurance Administration, storm facts from the National Weather Service, historic district details from National Register records and handling advice for papers from the National Archives. Where a local number is not available, we say that instead of inventing one.

If you find something that looks wrong, write to us at the email below and we will check it. We would rather correct a mistake than leave it standing. Corrections go on the page with a new date, so you can see what changed and when it changed.

What a first visit looks like

When a crew arrives, the first job is safety. The technician checks for electrical hazards, gas smells and structural problems before touching anything. Then comes a walk-through with a moisture meter and a thermal camera to find where the water went, which is often farther than it looks.

Only after that does the technician explain the plan. You hear which rooms are involved, what equipment will run, what has to be removed and roughly how long the drying will take. You also hear what the work is likely to cost, and you decide before the crew starts. If something changes during the job, we tell you before we act on it.

Our crews photograph the loss and record readings at the start and on each visit. You keep those records. They are the evidence of what was wet and how it dried, whether or not you ever file an insurance claim.

Learn before you call

The blog has fifteen guides on claims, costs, hidden leaks and belongings. They cite published sources and say what is local. If water is spreading right now, call instead of reading.

You can also email contact@catonsvillewaterdamagepros.com with a non-urgent question, though calling is faster for anything involving active water.
