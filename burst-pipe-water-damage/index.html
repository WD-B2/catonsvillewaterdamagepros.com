Burst pipe: what to do first

A burst supply line is different from a slow leak because the water is under pressure. A split in a half-inch line can release a lot of water in minutes. The first job is to stop the flow, and everything after that is about limiting how far the water traveled.

[01].

Shut off the main.

Find the main water valve, usually where the line enters the house, and turn it fully off. Then open a low faucet to drain what is left in the lines.

[02].

Check the electrical risk.

If the water reached outlets, appliances or the panel, do not stand in it. Cut power only from a dry spot or call the utility.

[03].

Protect what you can.

Lift furniture legs onto blocks, pull rugs and move electronics and papers to a dry room.

[04].

Photograph before moving.

Take photos and short videos of the pipe, the wet areas and damaged belongings for your insurer.

Then call a plumber for the pipe itself and call us for the water. Baltimore County says a permit is required for all plumbing and gasfitting work and that only a Baltimore County licensed plumber or gasfitter contractor can apply for it. Ask your plumber about that before the repair is scheduled.

Why pressurized failures spread faster than leaks

A burst pipe sprays or pours into the wall cavity where it sits. The wall below fills like a trough, and water runs along studs, through bottom plates and under baseboards into the next room. A leak above a ceiling can wet the ceiling below, the walls beside it and the floor underneath.

That path is why a technician checks with moisture meters and a thermal camera before deciding what to open. A wall that looks dry two feet from the break may hold water at the bottom plate. The visible stain is usually the smallest part of the problem.

Common causes of burst lines in older houses

Old galvanized or copper lines can corrode through. Poor solder joints and failed push fittings let go under pressure. Supply hoses to toilets and sinks can burst at the connection. A house built before 1950, as many in the Old Catonsville Historic District were, may still carry some original piping, so ask your plumber what material is behind your walls.

Cleanup, drying and repair after a burst

Step 01.

Extract the water.

Pumps and extractors remove what is standing. This is the same first step described under water extraction.

Step 02.

Open what is wet.

Baseboards come off and small cuts may be made in drywall so air can reach the cavity. Soaked insulation is removed.

Step 03.

Dry with equipment.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run while readings are logged. Equipment moves as the wet zone shrinks.

Step 04.

Repair the finish.

Drywall, paint, flooring and trim are replaced once the readings pass. The plumbing repair itself is the plumber's work.

Burst pipes often wet a kitchen or bathroom. If cabinets are involved, the kitchen water damage page covers what can be dried and what has to be replaced.

Clean water is still a problem after 48 hours

A burst supply line carries clean water. That is the best case, since the pad under carpet and some drywall may be dried instead of discarded. But clean water turns into a larger loss when it sits. Over a day or two it picks up bacteria from the materials it soaks, and a closed wall gives mold the moisture it needs.

That is why speed matters even when the water was clean. The faster the equipment goes in, the more of your house stays in place. A burst that is stopped and extracted the same day often needs far less rebuilding than one discovered after a weekend away.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration treats clean supply-line water as Category 1, and it also warns that the category can worsen with time. That is the reason a burst line is worth acting on the same day.

What drives the price of burst pipe cleanup

  • How long the pipe ran before it was shut off.
  • How many rooms and floors the water reached.
  • Whether the finished surfaces are drywall, plaster, hardwood or carpet.
  • Drying days and the number of air movers and dehumidifiers needed.
  • Rebuild scope after the walls and floors are verified dry.
